# em1 exemplars - the operator's own cold openers

**THESE ARE STRUCTURE. DO NOT COPY THE CONTENT.**

Every sentence below was written by the operator and sent to a real person at a
real company. They are here so the writer can see the SHAPE of an em1 that this
agency actually sends: how long it is, what order the five blocks come in, how
much of it is about them, where the give sits relative to the ask, and how small
the ask is.

**The prohibition is explicit and it is not a style note.** No phrase, clause,
sentence, number or proof from these exemplars may appear in generated copy.
Reusing a sentence from here would be writing another company's email to a
stranger - which is the 2026-09-23 incident this repository is still containing,
where 64 emails carried a different agency's pitch signed with the operator's
name. Every claim in generated copy must trace to that record's own evidence
through `claims.check`, and these exemplars license nothing.

**ANONYMISED, AND THE ANONYMISATION IS PART OF THE ARTEFACT.** The recipient
company and the recipient person are replaced by role placeholders, one token
for one token, so every word count below is the count of the ORIGINAL body. The
sending company and the operator's name stay: they are ours. Our own client
references are placeholders too - they are third-party companies and this file
is published the moment it is committed. The scan that proves it is a WHITELIST:
it asks which capitalised tokens are allowed to survive rather than spelling the
ones that are not, because a check that spells a forbidden name reintroduces it,
which happened three times in one night here.

The signature is a bare name, a title line and a URL with no sign-off line, which
is why `lint.countable_words` counted all 17 of these at their raw token count
until 2026-10-03 and credited every body with 7 words no prospect reads. The
contract's band of 90 to 140 with a target of 120 is derived from the column above
rather than chosen; `lint.WORD_CONTRACT` is its one authority.

## The five blocks

**1  WHO WE ARE, IN ONE CLAUSE**

One sentence naming the sending company and what it does for whom. Never two sentences, never a mission, never a capability list.

**2  A SPECIFIC, DATED THING ABOUT THEM**

A funding round, a hire, an interview, an award, a market move - something that happened, that they would recognise, and that a stranger could only know by looking. This is the whole personalisation budget and it is spent here.

**3  THE GIVE, BEFORE ANY ASK**

"Before pitching you anything" and then a concrete deliverable with its parts enumerated: how many accounts, scored how, which buying committee, what else arrives with it. It closes with permission to keep it regardless.

**4  PROOF AS PRECEDENT, NOT AS A BOAST**

One sentence: we started the same way with these clients, and here is the number. Named, countable, and it describes OUR work rather than their future.

**5  ONE ASK, ONE QUESTION, ONE LINE**

A single question, under ten words, naming the length of the meeting. Nothing after it but the signature.

Every one of the 16 exemplars carries all five, in that order, and nothing else.
No postscript, no second ask, no link, no attachment, no second question.

### The outlier - 69 words, not reproduced here

One of the seventeen messages is a reply inside an existing thread rather than a
cold opener. It carries none of the five blocks, it sits far below the em1 floor at
69 words, and its mail footer carries a phone number - so it is described here
rather than printed. It is named because the band above is derived from the 16
messages that ARE cold openers, and dropping one from the sample without saying so
would make the band look tighter than the evidence supports.
