# The seal on 487, 489 and 493 — 2026-10-02

TASK-936. Opened by operator decision 2026-10-01: *"487/489/493 na write gateu:
zasebni zadatak, otvori ga, ne sad."*

**Read this before changing anything about these three campaigns, and before
assuming any of them is protected by something it is not.**

## What was wrong

The standing belief was that 487/489/493 are untouchable. At the write layer
that was false. Measured on `d98c83ce` with a transport and a read-back that
**raise if they are contacted** — so the evidence is effect, not a log row —
`bison.resume` and `bison.assign_sender` passed ownership for all three and
**reached transport**.

What actually held them was three conditions, and the write gate was none of
them:

| condition | where it lives | how it changes |
|---|---|---|
| the killswitch | `killswitch.global_state`, derived from `push.LiveSendNotEnabled` existing | a code change |
| `sending.live=false` | a workspace policy row for `productive` | one `workspaces.set_policy` call |
| stale approvals | 0 of 3,005 stored approvals still valid | one re-approval |

Each is changeable independently of the write path, by somebody who is not
thinking about these three campaigns at all. None of the three is a diff
anybody reviews with 487 in mind.

`config/internal-campaigns.txt` (on `task-internal-campaign-guard`) says nothing
about them on purpose: it classifies **tenancy**, and all three are
`resonate_os`, which is a PASS there.

## What holds them now

`providerwrites._SEALED_CAMPAIGNS` — **one table**, three entries, each carrying
the provider id, the canonical row it is bound to, and the operator's recorded
reason. `require_not_sealed` is the **first** thing `_perform` does, ahead of
`describe` and ahead of `require_supported`. It reads no setting, no approval,
no killswitch and no network.

Three checks, because they are three different attacks:

1. **by provider id** — a write naming 487 is refused whatever row it claims to
   be acting for.
2. **by canonical row** — a write against `productive-email-control-v3` is
   refused even when its `bison_campaign_id` has been edited away. That is the
   defeat reproduced against this very row on 2026-09-18, when an unpinned
   allowlist let it be re-bound 487 → 327 and carried its grant onto a client
   campaign.
3. **by what canonical state says the row is bound to** — a row with an
   innocent name that resolves to a sealed campaign is a route to it.

The payload is walked for campaign-ish keys, so `{"campaign_id": 487}`,
`{"campaign_ids": [487]}`, `{"campaign": {"id": 487}}` and
`{"bison_campaign_id": 487}` are all found. A bare `id` is deliberately NOT
treated as a campaign: a lead payload carrying `{"id": 487}` must not produce a
refusal naming the wrong thing.

### One list, two enforcement points

The same table is read a second time at
`providers.refuse_sealed_campaign`, called from `refuse_unauthorized_write`
**ahead of `writes_allowed`**. That is not a second list — nothing is copied or
cached, `providerwrites.sealed_campaigns()` is read every time, and emptying the
table unseals both at once. Two points, because they see different evidence:
`perform` sees the caller's arguments and the canonical row, the wire check sees
the URL about to be opened.

It exists because **the door is not the only way out**, and because an open
`providers.allow_writes(...)` scope must not lift the seal —
`scripts/resume_487.py` wraps its one bare call in exactly that scope and, before
this change, that scope was the whole authorization it needed.

## The name-set diff, and the two traps it walked into

Compared against the authoritative master baseline,
`task-named-baseline:docs/state/SUITE-BASELINE-2026-10-02-FULL.json` — master
`10a38310`, 231 named failures, COMPLETE, 2717.8s. Method: the baseline's own —
`py -3 -m tests.offline -v`, one process, watchdog 3600s — compared by **set of
names**, never by count. Full record in
`docs/state/SUITE-TASK-936-2026-10-02.json`.

**Result: 231 of 231 baseline names reproduced, zero regressions, zero fixes.**
One extra name appears,
`test_autonomous_production_is_not_a_self_stamp.StagingActuallyConsultsIt.test_a_step_approved_autonomously_certifies_at_staging`,
and it is RED on master and RED on this branch identically when re-run
individually, back to back. It is absent from the baseline, so the baseline's own
full run did not record it. Not this change.

Two traps, both measured rather than reasoned about:

1. **The parse, not the branch.** The first diff reported *245 regressions and
   231 fixes* — every name on both sides, which is the signature of a spelling
   mismatch, not of a broken branch. The baseline spells a name
   `module.Class.test`; the first parser took the bare method name. The fix is a
   **positive control on the parse itself**: the two sides must share most of
   their names, and they now share 231 of 231.
2. **The UTC date boundary.** The first full run on this branch reported 15 new
   names. Seven were per-DAY counting tests — three in
   `TheCapCountsDurableRowsNotAPlanDict`, four in `TheGateRefusesTheSixthAccount`
   — and that run started at **23:52 UTC and crossed UTC midnight mid-suite**
   (01:52 local, CEST = UTC+2). Re-run individually they are green on both sides,
   and they do not appear in the second run, which stayed inside one UTC day.

The remaining seven were real, and every one of them was the seal working:
`test_a_reply_stops_the_other_channel` bound a synthetic row to provider campaign
487 and drove the real `leadstop` → `perform` path with `live=True`, and
`test_a_prompt_asking_nicely_is_not_a_security_boundary` used
`/api/campaigns/487/pause` as the one example URL for three cases that assert an
authorized write *reaches* the wire. Both fixtures now name an unsealed campaign;
the cases that assert a refusal keep 487 and now exercise both guards.
