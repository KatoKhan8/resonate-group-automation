## Part 2: The Approval Mechanism

### What is `approval.by`?

`approval.by` is the field on a cadence step that records **who approved it**. It is set by `approve_step` in `src/approve.py` when a human operator explicitly approves a step.

The approval stamp has three fields:
- `by`: the identifier of the approver (e.g., "operator-control-arm", "claude", "zb")
- `at`: the timestamp of the approval
- `fingerprint`: a SHA-256 hash of the step's text (channel + subject + body + note)

### Who can set it?

Any caller with access to `approve_step` can set it. The `by` parameter is a free-form string. In practice:
- `operator-control-arm`: a human operator approved the step through the CONTROL arm (the cohort ladder's CONTROL fallbacks)
- `claude`: the step was generated by Claude (the AI) and the approval records that fact
- A person's identifier (e.g., "zb"): a specific human approved the step

### What does `operator-control-arm` mean?

`operator-control-arm` means the step was approved by a human operator as part of the CONTROL arm of the cohort ladder. The CONTROL arm uses generic, non-personalized fallback text from the client config (`config/clients/productive.yaml` under `linkedin_sequence.fallbacks`).

When a step has `approval.by: "operator-control-arm"`, it means:
1. A human read the CONTROL fallback text
2. The human approved it for use
3. The fingerprint of the approved text matches the CONTROL fallback in the config

### What does `claude` mean?

`claude` means the step was generated by Claude (the AI) and the approval records that fact. It does **not** mean a human read and approved the generated text. It means the generation process completed and the step was stored.

A step with `approval.by: "claude"` has **not** been human-approved. It is a draft that was generated but not reviewed.

### The approval path

1. A step is generated (by Claude or by template expansion)
2. The step is stored in the record's cadence
3. A human operator reviews the step using `python -m src.approve pending`
4. The human approves the step using `python -m src.approve step --id <record> --contact <contact> --step <step> --by <identifier>`
5. The approval is recorded with the approver's identifier and the fingerprint of the text
6. If the text changes, the fingerprint no longer matches and the approval is invalidated

### The gap

Contact #1 (austin-ball at px-771db3f7cb5a) has `approval.by: "operator-control-arm"` for li1-li5. This means a human operator approved the CONTROL fallback text for all five steps.

However, **approval is still required**. The CONTROL fallbacks must be explicitly approved by a human operator before they can be staged. The approval path is:

1. Replace the generated copy in contacts 2 and 3's cadence steps with the CONTROL fallbacks
2. Approve each step using `python -m src.approve step --id <record> --contact <contact> --step <step> --by operator-control-arm`
3. The factory will then accept the copy for staging

### What is required

This is **both a data change and an approval change**:

1. **Data change:** Replace the generated copy in contacts 2 and 3's cadence steps with the CONTROL fallbacks from `config/clients/productive.yaml`. This is a write to `work/queue.jsonl` (or the snapshot, if working from a snapshot).

2. **Approval change:** Approve each step using the approval CLI. The approval records that a human operator reviewed and approved the CONTROL fallback text.

Without both steps, the contacts cannot be staged. The factory requires approval for every step, and the approval must match the text being sent.
