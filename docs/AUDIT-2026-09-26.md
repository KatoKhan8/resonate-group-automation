## Executive Summary

The codebase has substantial existing infrastructure covering most of the spec's ten sections. The recurring defect is **correct modules with no consumer**: `generate_campaign.py` (the v2 entrypoint), `secondbrain.py`, `campaignstrategy.py`, `offers.py`, and the five skills are all internally correct, individually tested, and currently produce zero effect on anything a real send would generate because `src/generate.py` — the actual production path — never imports them.

The safety layer is strong: copylint (30 tests, all pass), sequencegate (19 tests, all pass), eligibility (1113 lines, re-derives every verdict), spendledger (per-provider ceilings with reservation), and the provider write barrier (`providerwrites.SUPPORTED`) keep every gate fail-closed. No campaign is live or sending.

The three things the operator will want acted on first:

1. **The production entrypoint is disconnected.** `generate_campaign.py` has zero callers in `src/`. The Second Brain, the offer engine, the strategy cache, and the five skills feed into it and it feeds into nothing. TASK-400 is the fix.
2. **The email→LinkedIn cross-channel stop is wired, tested, sealed, and incapable.** `leadstop.py` reads `linkedin_url` from a store where 1,014 contacts carry `linkedin` and zero carry `linkedin_url`. The fix is in the code but `LINKEDIN_STOP_LEAD` is not in `SUPPORTED`.
3. **All six offers are `pending`.** The offer engine ships, the schema is right, and not one offer can reach copy generation until the operator approves commercial terms.

### Signal taxonomy

Three scopes: `ACCOUNT`, `PERSON`, `ENGAGEMENT`. 

Account-level signals: `FUNDING`, `HEADCOUNT_GROWTH`, `HIRING_SURGE`, `NEW_EXECUTIVE`, `NEW_MARKET`, `NEW_OFFICE`, `TECH_ADOPTION`, `TECH_REMOVAL`, `JOB_POSTING`, `WEBSITE_CHANGE`, `COMPANY_NEWS`, `EXPANSION`.

- Signal freshness: `weight_at()` returns the signal's current weight AND the multiplier that produced it, with per-type decay.

- Person-level signal relevance is the most important improvement the spec names. Currently a company-level hiring signal reaches every contact at that company regardless of role.

### What copylint checks

- Empty steps (step count must equal `STEPS_EXPECTED = 5`)
- Step 1 opens on a pack fact (traceability)
- Duplicate first lines across leads
- Banned phrases (shared with `lint.py`)
- Buzzwords (synergy, game-changer, leverage, etc.)
- Dash rule (em, en, spaced hyphen)
- Unrendered variables (`{` without `}`)
- Traceability: figures, dates, quoted phrases, proper nouns must appear in pack facts

### What sequencegate checks

- Repetition across email steps (lexical overlap with stopwords removed)
- Hypothesis stated as finding (`AS_FACT_RES` patterns)
- Cross-channel question repetition (LinkedIn asking what email already asked)
- One capability across a whole batch
- Duplicate thread subjects
- Blocking qualifications (UNQUALIFIED, INSUFFICIENT_DATA, DISQUALIFIED, HELD)
- Step 1 names no researched fact
- Over-long first email

### What is NOT checked (spec gaps)

- **Semantic repetition across five email steps.** The current check is lexical (content-word overlap). The spec asks for semantic validation ("five emails making one argument in five phrasings"). `sequencegate` reports paraphrase blindness as a known limitation: "the paraphrase blind spot is reported not silent."
- **Repetition between email and LinkedIn messages.** `cadence.cross_channel_leaks()` checks whether one channel NAMES the other without evidence. It does not check whether the LinkedIn sequence repeats the email's ARGUMENT.
- **Consistency with approved campaign offer.** No check verifies that generated copy aligns with the offer selected for the campaign. The v2 engine's strategy stage would provide this, but it is disconnected.
- **Unsupported commercial promises.** `outreachclaims` controls what a message may assert, but its claim types cover prior touches and relationships, not commercial terms.
- **Signal-to-person relevance.** No validation checks whether a signal mentioned in copy is relevant to the specific recipient.

### What should not be touched

- The lexical overlap checks in `sequencegate` — they are fast, deterministic, and the spec says to preserve them.
- `copylint`'s shared `BANNED_PHRASES` and `SUBSTITUTED_PUNCTUATION` with `lint.py` — single source of truth, tested.

### What works

- Small-sample protection: a cohort below the floor gets `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` and no recommendation.
- The default objective is positive replies, not any reply.
- Cross-workspace isolation is structural.

## ARCHITECTURAL RECOMMENDATIONS

### REC-1: Connect the v2 entrypoint to production

TASK-400 is the fix. Once `generate.py` calls or absorbs `generate_campaign.py`, the Second Brain, offers, strategy, and five skills become production-effective.

### REC-2: Add semantic repetition checking

The current lexical overlap check catches word-for-word repetition but not paraphrase. The spec asks for semantic validation. An LLM-based check (cheap model) comparing the argument of each step against the others would close this gap. `sequencegate` already reports this as a known limitation.

### REC-3: Add cross-channel argument repetition checking

`cadence.cross_channel_leaks()` checks whether one channel NAMES the other. A separate check should verify that the LinkedIn sequence does not repeat the email's argument. This is distinct from the lexical check and requires semantic comparison.

### REC-4: Build person-level signal relevance

The signal taxonomy distinguishes account vs person scope, but no module filters signals by whether they are relevant to the specific recipient's role. A hiring signal is relevant to a Head of Delivery but not necessarily to a finance contact.

### REC-5: Persist a learning memory

`learning.py` analyses cohorts but does not persist a learning history. The spec asks for structured storage of campaign performance, offer performance, objections, and strategy decisions with outcomes.

### REC-6: Build the curated example library

The spec asks for a Copywriting Skill that uses approved examples with metadata. No example library exists. The ladder system provides per-step objectives but not concrete examples of good and bad copy.

### REC-7: Add per-campaign cost tracking

Connect spend ledger entries to campaign IDs so the operator can see cost per campaign, cost per processed lead, and (when data is available) cost per qualified meeting.

### REC-8: Research freshness and reuse

Research packs are built per-run with no cross-process caching. A freshness policy (e.g., packs built within 7 days are reusable) would reduce redundant enrichment spend.
