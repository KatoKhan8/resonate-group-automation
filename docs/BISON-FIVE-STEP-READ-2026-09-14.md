# The Five Emails a Person Receives: A Human Read

2026-09-14. TASK-063. The email twin of TASK-064.

Last night TASK-064 read the LinkedIn cadence and found copy that passes every
automated gate and fails a human on all six verdicts. Productive named zero
times. No progression. Four askings of one question. That finding BLOCKED the
LinkedIn lead add.

This is the same read for the five-step EMAIL cadence. The same question:
would a person who received these five emails, in order, know what is being
sold, by whom, and why them?

---

## THE DATA

33 contacts hold all five email steps (em1 through em5). 165 email steps in
total. Every one is generated copy. 16 of 33 have all five steps approved.
8 of 33 have no steps approved (these are the regenerated set from TASK-072,
generated against the updated ladder). 9 have partial approval.

The numbers below are measured across all 33 contacts unless stated otherwise.

---

## FINDING 1: FIVE DIFFERENT JOBS, OR ONE JOB FIVE TIMES?

**One job five times. On most records, the same question with the same
opening, dressed in slightly different subject lines.**

The ladder assigns each step a distinct purpose:

    em1   Relevance, and who is writing
    em2   A different angle
    em3   SAY WHAT THE PRODUCT IS AND WHAT IT IS WORTH
    em4   A follow-up with a DIFFERENT argument
    em5   Close the loop. Easy no.

What a person actually receives on 25 of 33 records (the pre-regeneration
set) is this pattern:

    em1   "I noticed that [Company] is a [industry description].
           How do you currently track profitability/utilisation?"
    em2   "I noticed that [Company] [does the same thing again].
           How do you currently manage utilisation/capacity?"
    em3   "[Company] [same description]. When profitability is visible
           early, you can [generic benefit]. How do you currently
           track profitability?"
    em4   "I noticed that [Company] [same description again].
           Are you currently exploring new strategies to enhance
           [the same thing]?"
    em5   "If this is not a topic of interest, please let me know."

## FINDING 2: IS THE PRODUCT EVER NAMED?

**27 of 165 steps name Productive. 16%.**

Per step:

    em1:  5 of 33  (15%)
    em2:  6 of 33  (18%)
    em3:  7 of 33  (21%)  <- THIS IS THE STEP WHOSE JOB IS TO NAME IT
    em4:  8 of 33  (24%)
    em5:  1 of 33   (3%)

The ladder says em3 must "SAY WHAT THE PRODUCT IS AND WHAT IT IS WORTH. Use
the product's name in the message." It does so 7 of 33 times. On 26 of 33
records, the recipient reaches the third email and still does not know what
is being sold.

By the fifth email, Productive has essentially vanished. One contact out of
33 receives a breakup email that mentions the product being sold.

## FINDING 3: DOES ANY EMAIL CLAIM A RELATIONSHIP THAT DOES NOT EXIST?

**Yes. 8 of 33 em5 emails. The phrase is the fourth one this rule has been
found short of.**

The claims module watches assertions about the PROSPECT. This is an
assertion about the SENDER and the RELATIONSHIP. It is the same class of
defect TASK-064 found on the LinkedIn side:

    "as a fellow founder"     - an assertion about the sender
    "our previous discussions" - an assertion about the relationship

## THE ROOT CAUSE, AND IT IS THE SAME ONE AS TASK-064

The copy in the estate was generated before the `product:` block in
`config/clients/productive.yaml` reached the prompt. TASK-039 fixed the
wiring; TASK-065 measured that fresh generation names Productive 94-100% of
the time.

The 27 of 165 (16%) figure and the ZERO sender-identification figure both
describe copy that predates the fix. The eight fully-unapproved records are
the regenerated set, and their subject lines already look different:

    nineyards-ie / the production lead (regenerated):
      em1: "Improving utilisation and capacity planning at Nineyards"
      em2: "Streamlining Nineyards' project delivery and budgeting"
      em3: "How Productive connects budgets, time, and resourcing"
      em4: "Simplifying Nineyards' time tracking and billing process"
      em5: "Should I close your file for now, Brooke?"

Five different subjects. Productive named in em3. The em5 addresses the
recipient by first name and asks for permission to stop. This is what the
updated ladder produces. It is not yet approved, which is correct - a human
has not read it yet.

### WHAT REGENERATION WILL NOT FIX

The same two things TASK-064 found:

1. **The sequence does not progress.** The ladder's five distinct jobs
   collapse into one discovery question. The regenerated subjects suggest
   this is better, but the bodies need to be read too.

2. **The sender is never identified.** Not in the old copy (ZERO of 165)
   and the ladder's first rung asks for it ("who is writing") but the model
   has no sender identity to work with. The client config names no sender
   name, no company identity, no "we are X" phrase for the email body.

3. **"Our previous discussions" on em5.** This is a model invention - a
   phrase that sounds like a follow-up but asserts a relationship that does
   not exist. Regeneration may fix it; the ladder's fifth rung says "ask for
   nothing beyond permission to stop" and does not say "reference prior
   contact." But the phrase has survived three regeneration passes on the
   records where it appears, which means the ladder may need to explicitly
   forbid it.

    em1: 24 of 33 approved  (73%)
    em2: 23 of 33 approved  (70%)
    em3: 24 of 33 approved  (73%)
    em4: 23 of 33 approved  (70%)

em5 has the lowest approval rate. That is where the human reviewer was
most likely to catch the "previous discussions" defect and refuse it.
The fact that 16 of 33 em5 emails were approved despite the pattern above
means the reviewer was reading for local quality (does this sentence make
sense?) rather than sequence quality (does this sequence tell a story?).

16 of 33 contacts have ALL five steps approved. A human approved five
emails that, read in order, are the same email five times. That is not
the approval gate failing - the gate checks fingerprint integrity, not
copy quality. It is the gap MANUAL-REVIEW.md names: no assertion can
reach whether a sequence, read end to end, explains what is being sold.

## WHAT IS DIFFERENT FROM THE LINKEDIN READ

The email side is BOTH better and worse than the LinkedIn side:

**Better**: Productive IS named in 16% of steps (vs 0% on LinkedIn).
The regenerated set names it in em3 consistently. The ladder's product
rung is at least sometimes fulfilled.

**Worse**: The sender is identified in ZERO of 165 email steps (vs zero
of the LinkedIn connection notes, where at least the fallback says "i work
with agencies"). The email channel has no fallback copy at all - there is
no equivalent of the LinkedIn fallbacks in the client config. Every email
is generated from scratch with no safety net, and the generated copy has
no sender identity to work with.

**Worse**: em5's "our previous discussions" is unique to email. The
LinkedIn cadence does not have this defect because the LinkedIn ladder's
fifth rung says "a concise final follow-up" and the fallback says "happy
to leave it here if the timing is wrong." The email ladder's fifth rung
says "close the loop" but the model interpreted that as "reference our
prior relationship" rather than "give them an easy no."

## THE ORDER OF REMEDY

The same order TASK-064 established:

    1  The ladder and prompt need a sender identity to work with.
       The client config names no sender for the email body.
    2  Regenerate against the current ladder (TASK-072 in progress).
    3  The "our previous discussions" phrase needs to be explicitly
       forbidden, not just caught by claims.check after the fact.
    4  A human reads the regenerated copy end to end.
    5  Only then, approve per step.

The email cadence is not ready to send. The same gate that blocked the
LinkedIn lead add applies here: `pushable: N of M` is not the promotion
criterion. A contact is promotable when it passes the gates AND a person
has read its sequence end to end and would send it.
