## The Two Branches

A prospect walks ONE of these:

```
Already connected:   connected_1 -> connected_2 -> connected_3 -> connected_4
Not yet connected:   connection_note -> message_2 -> message_3 -> message_4
```

Both branches share li2..li4, so from position 2 onward they carry the same words. The only difference is the opening: a connection note (cold) vs. a first message (already connected).

## The Hand-Written Operator Fallbacks (The Quality Floor)

These are the words a person reads when a lead's own copy is missing. They are the only copy in this system written to be true of a stranger, and they are the standard the generated copy must beat.

| Role | Step | Fallback Text |
|------|------|---------------|
| connection_note | li1 | "hi, i work with agencies on project profitability and thought it would be good to connect." |
| connected_1 | li2 | "how do you currently get visibility on whether a project is making money while it is still running?" |
| connected_2 | li3 | "most agencies i speak to find that out at the end of a project rather than during it. is that how it works for you?" |
| connected_3 | li4 | "we built productive so budgets, time tracking and resourcing talk to each other. worth a look?" |
| connected_4 | li5 | "happy to leave it here if the timing is wrong. is there someone else who owns this?" |
| message_2 | li2 | (same as connected_1) |
| message_3 | li3 | (same as connected_2) |
| message_4 | li4 | (same as connected_3) |

**What the fallback sequence does:**
1. Who + why connect (no question that needs thought)
2. One operational question
3. A norm ("most agencies...") that reframes the question as a timing problem
4. **Names the product** and says what it does in one line
5. Easy out with a referral ask

This is a ladder. Each rung has a different job. The product is named on rung 4, after two questions have earned the right to offer something.

| message_2 (li2) | "how do you ensure profitability is visible in your operations from day one?" |

| message_4 (li4) | "i've seen how teams like yours can transform their approach to growth and culture. what challenges have you faced in aligning your brand with your business goals?" |

**Verdict:**

- **WHO is contacting them?** Not said. The connection note says "i admire how..." but never says who "i" is. No name, no company, no role. A stranger reading this has no idea who is writing.
- **WHY them?** "i admire how savage brands drives profitability and growth" - this is a compliment, not a reason. It could be sent to any company. There is no operational observation that says "I looked at YOUR company and noticed THIS."
- **WHAT problem is relevant?** Profitability visibility. But this is asked as a question in message 1, repeated as a statement in message 2, then abandoned in messages 3 and 4 for "growth and culture" and "navigating growth" - which are different problems entirely.
- **WHAT is Productive?** Never named. Never described. The recipient finishes four messages without learning what is being offered.
- **WHY could it matter?** Not said. No capability, no feature, no outcome that Productive delivers is mentioned.
- **WHAT is the next action?** Not said. Every message ends with a question, but none of them lead anywhere. There is no offer, no link, no reason to reply.

**Does the sequence progress?** No. It asks variations of the same question four times:

1. "how do you ensure profitability is visible?"
2. "how are you currently tracking your financial performance?"
3. "what challenges have you faced in aligning your brand with your business goals?"
4. "how savage brands is navigating growth?"

These are four different wordings of "tell me about your business." None of them build on the previous one. None of them introduce new information. A recipient could read any one of them in isolation and miss nothing.

**Compare to the fallback:**

The fallback sequence for this person would read:
1. "how do you currently get visibility on whether a project is making money while it is still running?"
2. "most agencies i speak to find that out at the end of a project rather than during it. is that how it works for you?"
3. "we built productive so budgets, time tracking and resourcing talk to each other. worth a look?"
4. "happy to leave it here if the timing is wrong. is there someone else who owns this?"

The fallback **names Productive**. The fallback **says what it does**. The fallback **gives an easy out**. The generated copy does none of these things.

**The fallback beats the generated copy on every dimension.**

## The Six Verdicts

### 1. Does the sequence progress?

**No.** Across all 15 contacts, the generated copy asks variations of the same question in every message. The word "profitability" appears in 100% of generated messages for founder-angle contacts. The sequence does not move from WHO -> PROBLEM -> WHAT PRODUCTIVE IS -> DIFFERENT ANGLE -> EASY OUT. It moves from "tell me about your profitability" to "tell me more about your profitability" to "tell me even more about your profitability" to "any thoughts on profitability?"

The ladder in `LINKEDIN_DEFAULT_LADDER` defines six distinct jobs:
1. Connection note: who + why
2. First message: one operational question
3. Second message: different angle, name the consequence
4. **SAY WHAT THE PRODUCT IS**
5. Final follow-up: one line, one question, no new argument
6. Close the loop: easy no

The generated copy executes rung 2 four times. Rung 4 (name the product) is never executed. Rung 6 (easy out) is replaced with "just checking in" or "i'd love to hear your thoughts."

### 2. Is the product named?

**No.** Across the three pushable contacts (12 generated messages total), "Productive" appears ZERO times. The product is never named, never described, never offered.

The fallback names Productive in 1 of 4 messages (connected_3/message_4: "we built productive so budgets, time tracking and resourcing talk to each other"). For the 12 non-pushable contacts whose sequences fire fallbacks on some steps, the recipient has a chance of learning what Productive is. For the 3 pushable contacts, that chance is zero.

**The estate baseline says li4 names Productive in 21 of 50 stored notes.** This campaign's pushable contacts name it in 0 of 12. The generated copy for this campaign is worse than the estate average.

### 3. Does the connection note say WHO IS WRITING and WHY CONNECT?

**No.** None of the 15 generated connection notes identify the sender. They all follow the pattern "hi [name], i admire how [company] [does something]. let's connect!" or "hi [name], as a founder, you know [something]. let's connect!"

The fallback - "hi, i work with agencies on project profitability and thought it would be good to connect" - is better. It says who (someone who works with agencies on project profitability) without naming a specific person, and it gives a reason to connect without asking a question that needs thought.

The generated notes also make assertions about the recipient that may not be true:
- "i admire how savage brands drives profitability and growth" - no evidence this is true
- "i admire how you focus on making profitability visible right away" - no evidence
- "as a fellow founder" (contact 8) - is the sender a founder? This is an assertion about the sender.
- "as a founder, you know the importance of having profitability visible on monday" - assumes the recipient agrees

### 4. Contextless discovery questions?

**Yes, in every generated message.** The connection note does not set up the first message's question. The first message does not set up the second. Each message is a standalone question that could be sent in any order.

Examples:
- "how do you currently ensure profitability is visible in your projects?" (contact 11) - No preceding context explains why this question is being asked.
- "how do you currently track profitability for your projects?" (contact 6) - Same.
- "how do you ensure profitability is visible in your operations from day one?" (contact 10) - Same.

The fallback's first message - "how do you currently get visibility on whether a project is making money while it is still running?" - is also a discovery question, but it follows a connection note that says "i work with agencies on project profitability," which provides context for why the question is being asked.

### 5. Do the two branches read as the same conversation?

**Yes, structurally.** Since li2 fills both connected_1 and message_2, the two branches share the same words from position 2 onward. A person who could have walked either branch would read the same messages starting from step 2.

However, the opening differs: the already-connected branch starts with a discovery question (connected_1), while the not-connected branch starts with a connection note. For the already-connected person, the first message is a cold question with no context. For the not-connected person, the first message comes after a connection note. This means the already-connected branch is actually WORSE - it opens with a question from a stranger who has provided no context.

### 6. Does the generated copy beat the fallbacks?

**No. The fallbacks beat the generated copy on every pushable record.**

| Contact | Generated | Fallback | Winner |
|---------|-----------|----------|--------|
| savagebrands-com (Paula) | 4 profitability questions, no product | Question -> norm -> product name -> easy out | **Fallback** |
| mischacommunications-com (Michelle) | 4 profitability questions, no product | Question -> norm -> product name -> easy out | **Fallback** |
| mypersonalestatesale-com (Jennie) | 4 profitability questions, no product | Question -> norm -> product name -> easy out | **Fallback** |

For non-pushable contacts, the mix of generated and fallback is better than pure generated (because the fallback names Productive), but still worse than pure fallback (because the generated messages repeat the same question).

The one exception is contact 7 (Tina Frost), whose generated copy uses different operational angles across the four messages. But even Tina's sequence never names Productive, so it still fails the ladder's most important rung.

## Summary of Findings

1. **The generated copy does not progress.** It asks the same question in different words across all four messages. The ladder's six distinct jobs are collapsed into one: "ask about profitability."

2. **Productive is never named in pushable copy.** 0 of 12 generated messages for the 3 pushable contacts name the product. The fallback names it in 1 of 4 messages.

3. **Connection notes never say who is writing.** All 15 generated notes follow "hi [name], i admire [company]..." without identifying the sender. The fallback is better.

4. **Every generated message is a contextless discovery question.** No message builds on the previous one. No message provides context for the next question.

5. **The two branches are structurally identical from step 2 onward.** This is correct by design, but the already-connected branch is worse because it opens with a question from a stranger.

6. **The fallbacks beat the generated copy on every pushable record.** The hand-written operator copy is the quality floor, and the generated copy falls below it.

## Root Cause

The model was not told what Productive is. The `product:` block in `config/clients/productive.yaml` exists and contains:

```yaml
product:
  name: Productive
  what_it_is: one place where an agency's budgets, time tracking, resourcing and invoicing talk to each other instead of living in separate tools
  capabilities:
    project_management: projects, tasks and delivery in one place
    time_tracking: time booked against the project and the budget it belongs to
    budgeting: what a project was quoted at and what it has burned so far
    resource_planning: who is booked on what next week, and where the next hire goes
    billing: invoices raised from the time and the budget rather than retyped
    profitability: margin per project while it is running, not after it closes
```

But the generated copy for this campaign was produced before this block was consumed by the prompt. The model had only `angle_wording` - two phrases for the persona - so every rung reached for the same one. The result is four messages asking about profitability in different words.

This is the same defect documented in the `product:` block's own comment: "The model was asked to write four LinkedIn messages, one of whose jobs is to say what the product does, on behalf of a product it had never been told about."

## Recommended Actions

1. **Do not push any of the 3 pushable contacts.** Their copy is worse than the fallback. Sending it would reach real people with four messages that never say what is being offered.

2. **Regenerate the LinkedIn copy for all 15 contacts** with the `product:` block in the prompt context. The ladder's rung 4 must name Productive and say what it does.

3. **Regenerate the connection notes** to identify the sender. "hi [name], i'm [sender] from [company]. i work with agencies on [angle]. thought it would be good to connect." is the minimum viable note.

4. **After regeneration, re-run this read.** The defect is in the copy, not the graph. The graph is correct; the words are wrong.

5. **The fallbacks remain approved.** They are better than the generated copy and can be used as-is until regeneration produces something that beats them.
